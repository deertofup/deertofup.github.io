Privacy Policy

txt — plain text editor · Last updated 6 September 2026

txt is a plain text editor for Android. This policy explains what the app does with data, in plain terms. The short version: the things you write stay on your device unless you switch on Google Drive sync yourself, in which case they go to your own Drive, and to no service of ours. If your device backs itself up to your Google account, that backup can include them too. Apart from the copies you send to your own Google account, the only data collected by anyone is what the advertising in the app requires.

What stays on your device

Your notes are ordinary UTF-8 .txt files written to the app's private storage on your phone. So are your bookmarks, your highlights, and your settings.

  • They are not uploaded to any server of ours. There is no account with us and nothing to sign in to.
  • They are not readable by other apps on your device.
  • Neither the developer nor any other app on your phone can read them.

A note leaves the app in three ways. Two of them you start: exporting it, which writes a copy wherever you pick, and switching on Google Drive sync. The third is Android's own backup, which belongs to the device rather than to this app; it is described under Keeping and deleting data below.

Google Drive sync

Sync is off until you turn it on. With it on, your notes are copied to a folder called txt in your own Google Drive, and changes travel in both directions so the same notes appear on your other devices.

  • The files go to your Drive account, not to the developer. There is no server in between. Nobody but you and Google can see them, exactly as with any other file in your Drive.
  • The app asks for one narrow permission, drive.file, which grants access only to files this app itself created. The rest of your Drive is not merely off limits to it — it is invisible to it.
  • What happens to the files once they are in Drive is governed by Google's own privacy policy and your Google account settings.
  • Pausing sync stops the copying; disconnecting also forgets the connection. Nothing is deleted either way: the notes stay on the phone and the folder stays in your Drive until you remove it yourself. You can also withdraw the permission at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

What is collected

The app shows advertising, supplied by Google AdMob. To do that, AdMob collects the following from your device:

DataWhy
Advertising ID and device identifiers To select advertising, limit how often the same advert is shown, and detect fraud
Your IP address, and the approximate location it implies — the city or region, never a precise position To select advertising suitable for where you are, and to detect fraud
Device and app information, and interactions with adverts To deliver and measure advertising
Performance diagnostics — how long the app takes to start, how much energy it uses To keep the advertising components working

The app requests no location permission and contains no location code; the approximate location above is what Google's advertising software infers from your IP address, as every advert-supported app does.

This collection is performed by Google's advertising software inside the app, and the data goes to Google. The developer of txt does not receive it, and does not operate any analytics of their own.

Purchases

Removing the adverts is a purchase made through Google Play. Payment, pricing, taxes and refunds are handled entirely by Google under the Google Play terms. The app never sees your payment details — it only learns whether a purchase completed, and stores that result on your device.

Your choices

  • Change your advertising consent, if you are in the EEA or the UK. A form from Google is shown there before the app requests its first advert, and your answer decides what the advertising may use. To answer differently later, open ad privacy choices at the foot of the app's settings. That row is shown only where the choice applies, so elsewhere it is not there.
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID in Android under Settings → Privacy → Ads. Deleting it stops apps, including this one, from receiving a personalised identifier.
  • Remove the advertising entirely from inside the app, either by watching a rewarded advert or by purchasing removal. With adverts removed, the advertising components stop requesting adverts.
  • Delete your data by deleting individual notes in the app, or by uninstalling it, which removes everything the app holds on the device. A copy in your Drive, or in your device's backup, outlives the app and has to be deleted where it lives.

Keeping and deleting data

Deleting a note deletes the file. Uninstalling the app removes all of its files from the device. Neither can be undone from inside the app, so export anything you want to keep first.

With sync switched on there is a second copy, in your own Drive. Deleting a note in the app removes it from that folder too on the next sync, and deleting it in Drive removes it from the app. Because the folder is yours, you can also delete it directly in Drive at any time, with or without the app installed. The developer holds no copy at all, and so has nothing to hand over or erase.

If your device has cloud backup switched on, Android may include this app's notes, bookmarks, highlights and settings in that backup, and in a transfer to a new phone. That is your device's backup, governed by your Android and Google account settings, and is not something the app runs or can read. It outlives uninstalling: install the app again on an account holding such a backup and Android may restore those notes into it. Removing that copy is done in your Android backup settings or in your Google account, not in the app.

Children
